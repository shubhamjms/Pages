A polished answer can feel complete before any useful work has happened. The real measure is whether a person can make a better decision, take a deliberate action, and inspect the evidence afterward.

## Fluency is an opening

Language models are exceptionally good at creating plausible continuations. That makes the first response useful for orientation, but insufficient as proof. A builder still needs to know which source shaped the answer, what assumptions remain, and what changed in the world.

> Treat the answer as an interface to the work, not as the work itself.

## Connect answer to action

An action should be small enough to explain. Name its inputs, permission boundary, expected result, and failure state. When those details are hidden, confidence becomes guesswork.

## Leave evidence behind

Evidence may be a citation, a changed record, a test result, or a clear statement that nothing was changed. It gives the next reader somewhere firm to begin.

### A useful sequence

1. Ask what decision needs support.
2. Gather the minimum trusted context.
3. Take the smallest authorized action.
4. Return the result and its evidence.
5. Leave a clear review point for a person.

The work after the answer is where trust becomes earned rather than implied.
